Title: "Prayer and fasting," or, The Cure of Unbelief (Matthew 17:19-21)

The disciples had cast out demons before, yet they failed with the epileptic boy. "Why could not we?" Jesus answered plainly: "Because of your unbelief."

**Failure has one cause.** In the spiritual world, power is received and held only by living faith in Christ. It is never a permanent possession of our own. Lack of faith remains Christ's explanation for weakness in his church.

**Faith needs a life of prayer to grow.** Some faith must come before prayer, but higher degrees of faith come only through much prayer. In adoring worship, silent waiting, and bringing God's Word back to him to speak it to us, the capacity to trust him grows. Men of strong faith are men of much prayer.

**Prayer needs fasting to be complete.** Prayer is the hand that grasps the unseen, and fasting is the hand that lets go of the seen. Food was humanity's first temptation, and fasting was Jesus' victory in the wilderness. At minimum this means temperance and self-denial. In times of intense desire, literal fasting expresses and deepens our readiness to sacrifice anything for God's kingdom.

**A wider separation.** Beyond food, the consecrated soul lays aside even lawful things that weigh it down, like a soldier carrying only what the battle requires.

The prize, "nothing shall be impossible to you," is worth the price.
