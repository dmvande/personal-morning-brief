Title: "Prayer and fasting," or, The Cure of Unbelief (Matthew 17:19-21)

The disciples had cast out demons before, yet they failed with the epileptic boy and were shamed before the crowd. "Why could not we?" Jesus answered plainly: "Because of your unbelief."

**Failure has one cause.** Their authority over demons was not a permanent possession of their own. It lived in Christ and could be held and used only by living faith in him. In the spiritual world, failure has one cause: unbelief. It remains Christ's explanation for weakness in his church.

**Why did their faith fail?** Faith is the simplest and also the highest exercise of the spiritual life, and it depends on that life's health. Faith that overcomes stubborn resistance belongs to people in close fellowship with God and separated from the world, through prayer and fasting.

**Faith needs a life of prayer to grow.** Some faith must come before prayer, yet higher degrees of faith come only through much prayer. "According to your faith be it unto you": faith varies between people and seasons, and it determines the measure of blessing. In adoring worship, silent waiting and bringing God's Word back to him to speak it to us, our capacity to trust him grows. Many cannot imagine spending hours with God, yet people of strong faith are always people of much prayer.

**Prayer needs fasting to be complete.** Prayer is the hand that grasps the unseen, and fasting is the hand that lets go of the seen. Food was humanity's first temptation in Eden, and fasting was Jesus' victory in the wilderness. At minimum this means a life of moderation and self-denial. In times of intense longing, literal fasting expresses and deepens our readiness to sacrifice anything, even ourselves, for God's kingdom.

**A wider separation.** Many Christians keep everything not forbidden. The consecrated soul lays aside even lawful things that weigh it down, like a soldier who carries only what the battle needs.

The prize, "nothing shall be impossible unto you," is worth the price.
