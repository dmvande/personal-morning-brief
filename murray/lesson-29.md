Title: "According to his will," or, Our Boldness in Prayer (1 John 5:14-15)

A major hindrance to believing prayer is uncertainty about whether a request is God's will. John meant the opposite: to give us boldness and assurance.

**God's will can be known.** The mistake is not believing we can know God's will, or not taking the time to find it. In prayer we deal with God's will as revealed in his Word, not his secret counsels. His general promises show his purposes. Faith applies a promise to a specific situation and asks with confidence.

**Word and Spirit together.** Applying promises is spiritual discernment, not mere logic, and each believer has a particular calling. The indwelling Spirit gives the wisdom to know what God has prepared for us. Some seek God's will in feelings without the Word, and others in the Word without the Spirit. The two must meet in a heart and life wholly yielded to both. Whoever does God's will learns to pray according to it.

**Stop blaming God's secret will.** "Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss." When answers are withheld, the Father is showing us that something is wrong in our aim, our faith or our life, so that he can teach us. Hiding behind "maybe it isn't his will" defeats his purpose.

Let Christ's words dwell in us richly and yield to the Spirit's anointing. The Father longs for his child to know his will and to pray boldly.
