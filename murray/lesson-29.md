Title: "According to his will," or, Our Boldness in Prayer (1 John 5:14-15)

A major hindrance to believing prayer is uncertainty about whether a request is God's will. Many read John's words as a reason for doubt, as if God's will were a hidden counsel no one can know, so the most they can offer is the prayer of submission. John meant the opposite. He wrote to give boldness: "we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him."

**God's will can be known.** The mistake is not believing we can know God's will, or not taking the time and trouble to find it. In prayer we deal with God's will as revealed in his Word, not his secret counsels, which we usually misjudge. His general promises reveal his purposes for his people and the world. Faith applies a promise to a specific situation and asks with confidence. John's own example follows: pray for a brother who sins, and God will give him life.

**Word and Spirit together.** Applying the promises takes spiritual discernment, not mere logic, and each believer has a particular calling and measure of grace. The indwelling Spirit gives the wisdom to know what God has prepared for us. Some seek God's will in inner feelings without the Word. Others seek it in the Word without the Spirit's living leading. The two must meet in a heart and life wholly yielded to both. Whoever lives in God's will by doing it learns to pray according to it.

**Stop blaming God's secret will.** "Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss." When answers are withheld, the Father is showing us that something is wrong in our aim, our faith or our life, so that he can teach us to pray rightly. Hiding behind "maybe it isn't his will" defeats his purpose and does great harm.

**The way forward.** Let Christ's words dwell richly in us, live each day under the Spirit's anointing, and abide in Christ in the Father's presence. The Father longs for his child to know his will and to pray boldly.
