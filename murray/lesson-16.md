Title: "Speedily, though bearing long," or, The Power of Persevering Prayer (Luke 18:1-8)

Why must a loving God who longs to bless be asked again and again, sometimes for years? When answers are delayed, the flesh finds it easy, and it looks like pious submission, to stop praying and assume God has secret reasons for withholding. Faith overcomes this temptation.

**Prayer accumulates.** Water must be gathered before it comes down in full force. A farmer takes ten thousand steps and sows ten thousand seeds before the harvest. In the same way, no believing prayer is lost. Each is stored up in heaven and works toward the answer. Abraham hoped against hope for years and through faith and patience inherited the promise.

**Two words describe God.**
- *He will answer speedily.* The blessing is ready, and his love longs to give it. He won't delay one moment longer than necessary.
- *He is long-suffering.* Like a farmer waiting for the early and latter rain while a child grabs half-ripe fruit, the Father knows when a soul or a church is ripe to receive and keep the blessing.

**Patience and faith together.** Faith holds the answer as already given in the promise and praises God for it. Patience keeps crying day and night until it is seen. Riper faith grows through confident, persistent prayer, and there may be things in us, in others or in the unseen world that must be set right first.

**Give God time.** Don't let delay suggest the request isn't his will, if it rests on his Word and the Spirit's leading. First the blade, then the ear, then the full grain. Every believing prayer brings the victory nearer. Persevering prayer also searches the heart, uncovers hindrances and draws us closer to Christ.

**Pray this way for the church.** She is like the helpless widow awaiting her absent Lord. Keep praying for the Spirit's mighty working upon her, and do not faint. "He will avenge them speedily."
