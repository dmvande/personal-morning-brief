Title: "Whose is this image?" or, Prayer in Harmony with the Destiny of Man (Matthew 22:20; Genesis 1:26)

The image on a coin settled who it belonged to. In the same way, the image we bear decides our destiny. Made in God's image, we belong to God, and prayer is part of that likeness: an earthly reflection of the loving fellowship within the Trinity.

**Man's original calling.** To understand prayer's power, we must ask what man was meant to be. God made humanity to fill, subdue and rule the earth as his representative. A viceroy advises his king, and while he is trusted his advice is acted on. In the same way, heaven was to bless the earth at man's request. The history of the world was placed in man's hands, his will and his prayer.

**Image made rule possible.** God could entrust such authority to man because of an inner likeness to himself. Man was fitted to be prophet, priest and king between God and creation: interpreting God's will, representing creation's needs, and receiving and passing on God's bounty. Though sin interrupted God's plan, prayer remains the proof of our likeness to God and the power that may move the hand that holds the universe. It is not only a beggar's cry for mercy but the highest exercise of a will made for divine purposes.

**Abraham shows it beginning again.** He is never recorded praying for himself. His prayers for Sodom and Lot, for Abimelech and for Ishmael shaped the lives of those around him.

**Christ restores it fully.** What the first Adam lost, the second has won back. "Ask what ye will" belongs first not to those seeking blessings for themselves but to fruitful branches who abide in Christ and live wholly for the Father's kingdom. Such believers act as priests and kings, each in their own circle.

The church's calling is higher than it knows. God wants redeemed people, not content merely to be saved, to share in determining the earth's history through prayer.
