Title: "After this manner pray," or, The Model Prayer (Matthew 6:9)

Every teacher knows the power of example. Jesus gave us the very words to bring to the Father: a prayer simple enough for a child, rich enough to hold all God can give, and the pattern for every other prayer.

**"Our Father which art in heaven."** No Old Testament saint dared address God as Father. This word holds the mysteries of redemption, new birth and faith. Knowing God's Fatherliness is both the first and the last lesson in prayer, and real prayer begins in a personal, loving relationship with him.

**God's interests come first.** We usually bring our needs first. Jesus reverses the order: Thy name, Thy kingdom, Thy will, and only then give, forgive, lead. The sooner we forget ourselves in seeking God's glory, the richer the blessing. Christ's school especially trains intercessors. A small child asks only for itself, but a grown son who manages his father's business asks largely and receives.

**The three "Thy" petitions.** *Hallowed be thy name*: the Father-name is the New Testament's central word, and God himself must reveal its holiness in us by his Holy Spirit. *Thy kingdom come*: like a king's heir in wartime, God's children long for the kingdom, and its coming waits on their prayers. *Thy will be done*: not only enduring suffering but heaven's glad, ready obedience.

**The three "us" petitions.** *Daily bread*: one who lives for the Father's interests can trust him fully for earthly needs. *Forgive us*: forgiveness must be real, not a formality. Only what is truly confessed is truly forgiven, and it requires a heart that holds nothing against anyone. *Lead us not into temptation*: we ask to be kept from sin and the evil one by the indwelling Spirit.

The heart that begins with the God-devoted "Thine" gains power to say "our." The prayer ends where it began, with God: "Thine is the kingdom, the power and the glory."
