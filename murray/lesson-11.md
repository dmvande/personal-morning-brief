Title: "Believe that ye have received," or, The Faith that Takes (Mark 11:24)

This promise is so large that our hearts try to shrink it. Murray urges us to let it enlarge our faith.

**"All things whatsoever."** Don't weaken Christ's words with cautious qualifiers. The only condition is real faith, which first seeks God's will through his Word and Spirit. Once we truly believe, nothing is impossible.

**Faith grows in prayer.** Faith comes before prayer, but it also grows out of it. In Christ's presence our motives are tested, the Spirit guides us, and faith rises to grasp what seemed too high. Don't wait to feel faith before praying. It is given most surely at the foot of the throne.

**The prayer of trust and the prayer of faith.** In ordinary matters we tell God our desires and leave the outcome to him, and his peace guards our hearts. The prayer of faith stands on a promise brought home by the Spirit and expects exactly what it asks.

**"Have received," now.** As with receiving pardon, we take the answer by faith before we see it, holding it with thanksgiving.

**"Ye shall have them."** Sometimes the blessing appears at once. Sometimes, as with Elijah praying seven times for rain, faith must persevere until the gift appears on earth.

It is Jesus who says this. He is the author of our faith and gives the spirit of faith to every childlike believer.
