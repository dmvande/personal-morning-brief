Title: "Believe that ye have received," or, The Faith that Takes (Mark 11:24)

This promise is so large that our hearts try to shrink it to what seems safe. Murray urges us instead to let it enlarge our faith. Faith is not just agreeing that God's Word is true. It is the ear that has heard God say what he will do and the eye that has seen him doing it. Like Solomon, we praise the God whose hand fulfills what his mouth has spoken.

**"All things whatsoever."** Reason objects that this can't be literally true and adds qualifiers like "if expedient." Jesus used the strongest words he could find, and said the same elsewhere: "All things are possible to him that believeth." The only condition is real faith, which first seeks God's will through his Word and Spirit. Once we truly believe, nothing is impossible. Weakening the promise weakens faith.

**Faith grows in prayer.** Faith comes before prayer, but it also grows out of it. In Christ's presence our motives are tested, the Spirit guides us, and faith rises to grasp what first seemed too high. Don't wait to feel enough faith before praying. The Spirit of faith is given nowhere so surely as at the foot of the throne.

**The prayer of trust and the prayer of faith.** In countless daily matters we tell God our requests and leave the outcome to him, and his peace guards our hearts. The prayer of faith goes further. Standing on a promise the Spirit has brought home, it expects exactly what it asks, as Jesus said of the mountain cast into the sea.

**"Have received," now.** As with receiving pardon, we take the answer by faith before we see it. We hold it and thank God that it is ours.

**"Ye shall have them."** Sometimes the blessing breaks through at once. Sometimes faith must persevere, as Elijah prayed seven times for rain God had already promised. Faith says, "I have it." Patience keeps praying until it is seen.

It is Jesus who says this. He lived this life of faith, and he gives its spirit to every childlike believer.
