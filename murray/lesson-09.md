Title: "Pray the Lord of the harvest," or, Prayer Provides Labourers (Matthew 9:37-38)

Jesus usually left his disciples to discern what to pray, but here he gave a specific request: ask the Lord of the harvest to send out labourers.

**Prayer is a real power.** Why should disciples pray for something God already sees? Couldn't Jesus pray it himself? The answer opens one of the deepest mysteries of prayer. Jesus spoke because he truly believed their prayer was needed and would help. He was moved with compassion for crowds "as sheep having no shepherd." God had given Abraham, Moses and Samuel authority to call on heaven's help for his work, and the same work was now passing into the disciples' hands. The supply of workers really does depend on the church's prayer.

**Two reasons we neglect it.** First, we lack Jesus' compassion. When believers accept the perishing as a charge entrusted to them, and not merely as a field of work, pity will soon drive them to cry, "Lord, send labourers." Second, we lack faith that prayer brings definite results. We do not live close enough to God, or wholly enough given to his kingdom, to be sure our prayer avails.

**Two blessings to ask for.** First, more men and women wholly given to God's service as ministers, missionaries and teachers. It is a blot on the church when such workers cannot be found. The exalted Christ gives Spirit-filled people as his chief gifts, and believing prayer moves the church to find the people and the means. Second, that every believer, redeemed for service, would serve. Wherever helpers are lacking, in a Sunday school, Bible class or rescue work, prayer has a promise of supply.

**Set apart time for this.** It draws us into Christ's compassionate heart. It shows us our royal position, whose will truly counts with God. It makes us real fellow-workers, sharing both his travail and his satisfaction when blessing comes that otherwise would not have come.
