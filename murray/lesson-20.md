Title: "That the Father may be glorified," or, The Chief End of Prayer (John 14:13)

Jesus answers prayer "that the Father may be glorified in the Son." This is the purpose of every answer he gives, and where it cannot be served he will not answer. So it must be the soul of our asking too.

**It was Jesus' own aim.** "I seek not mine own honour." Even his request to be glorified in heaven rested on having glorified the Father on earth, and was so that he could glorify the Father more. When we share this aim, our prayer cannot fail, because nothing glorifies the Father more than the Son doing what we ask.

**A searching word.** This cuts like a two-edged sword. Self-interest and self-will often drive our prayers more than any conscious longing for God's glory. That is why so many earnest prayers for children, friends and work go unanswered: our own joy outweighed God's glory.

**It can't be summoned at prayer time.** We can't switch on a desire for God's glory just before praying. "Do all to the glory of God" and "ask all to the glory of God" are twin commands. A life given to God's glory is the condition of prayer that glorifies him. This is simply right: creation exists for his glory, and outside it is only darkness.

**Self-seeking makes faith impossible.** "How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another?" Giving up our own glory awakens the spiritual sensitivity that is faith. Whoever seeks God's glory will see it in the answer to prayer.

**How to get there.** We cannot attain this by effort. Confess how little God's glory has been our passion, and let the Spirit show it to us. Look to Jesus, who glorified the Father by dying and was glorified through death. Trust him to live that self-denying life in us.

Then we can pray with quiet confidence and growing freedom: "Father, you know we ask it only for your glory."
