Title: "That the Father may be glorified," or, The Chief End of Prayer (John 14:13)

Jesus answers prayer "that the Father may be glorified in the Son." This is the purpose of every answer, so it must be the soul of our asking too.

**It was Jesus' own aim.** "I seek not mine own honour." Even his request to be glorified in heaven was so that he could glorify the Father more. When we share this aim, our prayer cannot fail.

**A searching word.** Often self-interest and self-will drive our prayers more than any longing for God's glory. That is why so many earnest prayers for children, friends and work go unanswered. Our joy outweighed God's glory.

**It can't be summoned at prayer time.** We can't switch on a desire for God's glory just before praying. "Do all to the glory of God" and "ask all to the glory of God" go together. A life given to God's glory is the condition of prayer that glorifies him.

**Self-seeking makes faith impossible.** "How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another?" Giving up our own glory awakens real faith.

**How to get there.** Confess how little God's glory has been our passion. Look to Jesus, who glorified the Father through death, and trust him to live that self-denying life in us.

Then we can pray with quiet confidence: "Father, thou knowest we ask it only for thy glory."
