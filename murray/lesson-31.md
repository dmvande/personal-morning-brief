Title: "Pray without ceasing," or, A Life of Prayer (1 Thessalonians 5:16-18)

The parable of the persistent widow teaches perseverance for one specific blessing. The epistles' call to "pray without ceasing" describes a whole life of prayer, in which the soul is continually rising to God in dependence, desire and expectation. Murray's closing lesson gathers what such a life needs.

**A life given to God's glory.** Trying to pray constantly in order to become very pious never works. Forgetting ourselves and living for God's kingdom enlarges the heart, until everything around us becomes a reason to call on God. We become watchmen on Zion's walls.

**Confidence that prayer works.** Jesus' teaching on prayer begins and ends with "Ask, and ye shall receive." Once we are sure our prayers really count, we dare not neglect them. Prayer then becomes a joy and second nature rather than a burden. The hindrance is never in God's promises but in us.

**The Spirit within.** Strong desire and firm faith are the Spirit's life in us, sometimes in wordless groanings, sometimes in clear requests for ourselves, for others and for the world.

**Above all, Jesus teaches by sharing his own prayer life.** He is our head and our life, and he takes us into his intercession before the Father. Our aim is not to work much and pray enough to keep the work right, but to pray much and work enough for prayer's blessing to reach others.

Between "rejoice evermore" and "in everything give thanks," unceasing prayer is a foretaste of heaven.
