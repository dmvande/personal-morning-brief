Title: "Pray without ceasing," or, A Life of Prayer (1 Thessalonians 5:16-18)

The parable of the persistent widow teaches perseverance for one specific blessing. The epistles' calls to "continue in prayer" and "pray without ceasing" describe a whole life of prayer, in which the soul is always rising to God in dependence, longing and expectation. Murray's closing lesson gathers what such a life needs.

**A life given to God's glory.** Trying to pray constantly in order to become very pious never works. Forgetting ourselves and living for God's kingdom enlarges the heart, until everything around us becomes a need for God's help and an opportunity for his glory. We become watchmen on Zion's walls, the Lord's remembrancers, whose cry truly moves the King.

**Confidence that prayer works.** Jesus' teaching on prayer begins and ends with "Ask, and ye shall receive." Once we are settled that our prayers really count, we dare not neglect them. God takes time because we live under the law of growth, but no prayer of faith is lost. Prayer then becomes a joy and a second nature rather than a burden. The hindrance is never in God's promises or his secret will, but in us.

**The Spirit within.** This union of strong desire and firm faith is the Holy Spirit's life in us. Sometimes it comes in wordless groanings, sometimes in clear assurance. It may be a plea for a deeper knowledge of Christ, or intercession for a soul, a work, the church or the world.

**Above all, Jesus teaches by sharing his own prayer life.** Seeing Jesus pray made the disciples ask to be taught. He is our head and our life, and he takes us into his intercession before the Father. Our aim is not to work much and pray enough to keep the work right, but to pray much and work enough for prayer's blessing to reach others.

Between "rejoice evermore" and "in everything give thanks," unceasing prayer is the power of eternal life on earth and a foretaste of heaven, where Jesus always prays.
