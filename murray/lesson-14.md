Title: "When ye stand praying, forgive," or, Prayer and Love (Mark 11:25)

Right after his great promise about believing prayer, Jesus adds a condition: forgive. "Have faith in God," which comes before the promise, sets our relationship with God right. "Forgive," which follows it, sets our relationships with people right. Prayer from a heart wrong on either side cannot prevail.

**A recurring teaching.** Jesus returned to this often: leave your gift at the altar and be reconciled first; "forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors"; the parable of the unmerciful servant. He seems to have seen that failing in love was the great sin even of praying people and a chief cause of weak prayer.

**Forgive as God forgives.** Every answered prayer rests on God's pardoning love. If he dealt with us by our sins, no prayer could be heard. When that love fills the heart, it flows out to others. In serious injuries we resist wounded pride, insisting on our rights and wanting the offender paid back. In daily annoyances we stop excusing hasty tempers and sharp words as "only human." Our forgiving love is the evidence of God's forgiving love in us.

**Daily life is the test of prayer.** Life is a whole, not loose pieces. God judges our prayer not by the mood we work up while praying but by who we are when not praying. Even unloving thoughts and judgments we let pass unnoticed can hinder prayer.

**Love is the soil of faith.** Love for others is the evidence of love for God and gives confidence before him: "If our heart condemn us not, then have we confidence toward God." In working and praying for others, we must see Jesus even in the unlovely and carry them in real, self-sacrificing love.

**Let it search us.** "Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss." What is needed is not perfect love but a fixed purpose to love as God loves, toward the evil and the unworthy too. Suffering, forbearing love prevails with God.
