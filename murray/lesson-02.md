Title: "In spirit and truth," or, The True Worshippers (John 4:23-24)

Jesus' first recorded teaching on prayer, given to the woman of Samaria, shows that the Father is actively seeking worshippers. Our worship gives joy to his loving heart. Yet he seeks true worshippers, and he finds many who are not what he desires. One of our first lessons, then, is to understand what worship in spirit and truth is and how to attain it.

**Three kinds of worship.** Jesus contrasts the Samaritans' ignorant worship and the Jews' informed worship with a new worship "in spirit and truth." This means more than sincerity or earnestness. There were earnest seekers among both groups. The same three kinds exist among Christians today: some pray earnestly with little understanding, others pray with knowledge and zeal, yet only Christ brings us into the third kind.

**As God is, so his worshipper.** Just as the eye is fitted for light and the ear for sound, the worshipper must be in harmony with the God he worships. Because God is Spirit, worship can no longer be confined to a mountain, a temple, an hour or a form. Many pray earnestly in church or in private, then live the rest of the week in a different spirit. Our whole life must become worship.

**The Spirit comes from God.** Only God can give Spirit. Christ came to baptize with the Holy Spirit, and once glorified he sent the Spirit of Sonship, by whom we cry "Abba, Father." This is why Jesus speaks of the Father: no Old Testament saint called God "my Father." True worship is the worship of children.

**"In truth" means reality.** In the Old Testament all was shadow and promise. In Christ the substance has come, and the Spirit of truth makes it our living experience.

**Our posture.** Confess that we cannot bring God the worship he seeks, wait teachably, and yield to the Spirit's breathing. Knowing God's infinite Fatherliness is the secret of prayer in spirit and truth.
