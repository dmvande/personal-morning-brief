Title: "I know that Thou hearest me always," or, Prayer in Harmony with the Being of God (John 11:41-42; Psalm 2:7-8)

A child can pray with simple faith, but God also wants us to love him with our minds. A hidden difficulty troubles many: how can a perfect, independent and unchanging God be moved by prayer? Is prayer only a help to us?

**The answer lies in the Trinity.** If God were a solitary person, influence would be impossible. But within God are Father and Son, joined by the Spirit. Their relationship is one of giving and receiving: "Ask of me, and I shall give thee." The Son's asking is a real movement of divine love. In God, nothing is done without the asking of the Son and the giving of the Father.

**Jesus' prayers on earth continue his asking in heaven.** "I knew that thou hearest me always" flows from his eternal Sonship. His prayer as a man links the Son's eternal asking with ours.

**God's decrees leave room for prayer.** The Son, as head of creation, always had a voice in the Father's counsels, and so do all who come through him. God lives in an eternal present, so his unchanging purpose fits with his freedom to be moved by prayer to do what he otherwise would not.

**Our comfort.** God's decrees are not an iron cage. Prayer's power comes from the fellowship of Father and Son, and in union with the Son our prayers share in the life of the Trinity: "Through him we have access by one Spirit unto the Father."
