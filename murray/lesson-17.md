Title: "I know that Thou hearest me always," or, Prayer in Harmony with the Being of God (John 11:41-42; Psalm 2:7-8)

A child can pray with simple faith, but God also wants us to love him with our minds and grow into the wisdom of his ways. A hidden difficulty troubles many: how can a perfect, self-sufficient and unchanging God be moved by prayer? Is answered prayer only a kindness to our weakness, and prayer's real benefit merely its effect on us?

**The answer lies in the Trinity.** If God were a solitary person, influencing him would be unthinkable. But within God are Father and Son, joined by the Spirit. Their relationship is one of giving and receiving: "Thou art my Son … Ask of me, and I shall give thee." The Father chose not to act alone in his counsels. The Son's asking is no empty show but a real movement of divine love. In the being of God, nothing is done without the asking of the Son and the giving of the Father.

**Jesus' prayers on earth continue his asking in heaven.** "I knew that thou hearest me always" flows from his eternal Sonship. His prayer as a man links the Son's eternal asking with the prayers of people on earth.

**God's decrees leave room for prayer.** The Son, as head and heir of all things, always had a voice in the Father's counsels. Room was left for him as Mediator, and so for all who come through him. God does not live in time with a fixed past binding him. In his eternal present, his unchanging purpose fits perfectly with his freedom to be moved by prayer to do what he otherwise would not have done.

**Our comfort.** How divine sovereignty and human freedom fit together remains a mystery beyond us. But God's decrees are not an iron framework that prayer struggles against in vain. He is living Love, keeping himself free to give every prayer its place in his rule of the world. In union with the Son, our prayers share in the life of the Trinity: "Through him we have access by one Spirit unto the Father."
