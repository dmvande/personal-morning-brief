Title: "If two agree," or, The Power of United Prayer (Matthew 18:19-20)

Jesus first taught private prayer behind a closed door. Here he adds united prayer with a special promise. Like a tree whose roots are hidden in the ground while its trunk grows into the sunlight, prayer needs both solitude with God and fellowship with others gathered in Jesus' name.

**We are one body.** Grace renews our bond with one another as well as with God. We say "Our Father," not only "My Father." It would be strange for children always to approach their father separately and never together. Believers are members of one body, and the Spirit shows his full power through their union, as when he came on the hundred and twenty praying with one accord.

**Three marks of true united prayer.**
- *Agreement on a specific request.* Not a general willingness to agree with whatever another asks, but a definite shared desire. This clarifies what we are asking, whether it is God's will, and whether we believe we have received it.
- *Gathering in Jesus' name.* His name is the centre that unites us, like a home that holds a family. To meet in his name is to have him present.
- *The sure answer.* "It shall be done for them of my Father." Jesus meant the prayer meeting as a means of securing answers, not only fellowship. When we feel too weak to believe alone, others help us.

**Paul counted on it.** He asked the Romans to strive together with him in prayer, and he told the Corinthians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians and Thessalonians that his deliverance, open doors and boldness depended on their prayers. He treated the church's prayers as a real factor in the work of the kingdom.

**Imagine the possibilities.** Think of husbands and wives, friends, congregations and the whole church praying in unity for the Spirit's outpouring. God rules the world through his saints' prayers, and every gathering in Jesus' name can be a gate of heaven.
