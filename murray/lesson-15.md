Title: "If two agree," or, The Power of United Prayer (Matthew 18:19-20)

Jesus first taught private prayer behind a closed door. Here he adds united prayer. Like a tree with hidden roots and a trunk reaching into the sunlight, prayer needs both solitude with God and fellowship with others.

**We are one body.** Grace renews our bond with one another as well as with God. We say "Our Father," not only "My Father." The Spirit shows his full power in the union of believers, as when he came on the hundred and twenty praying with one accord.

**Three marks of true united prayer.**
- *Agreement on a specific request.* Not general consent, but a definite shared desire. This clarifies what we ask and whether we can ask it in faith.
- *Gathering in Jesus' name.* His name is the centre that unites us, and his living presence among praying disciples gives united prayer its power.
- *The sure answer.* "It shall be done for them of my Father." A prayer meeting without recognized answers should be an oddity.

**Paul counted on it.** Paul repeatedly asked the churches to strive together with him in prayer for deliverance, open doors and boldness. He treated their prayers as a real factor in his ministry.

**Imagine the possibilities.** Think of husbands and wives, friends, congregations and the whole church praying together in faith. God rules the world through his saints' prayers, and every gathering in Jesus' name can be a gate of heaven.
