Title: "Pray to thy Father, which is in secret," or, Alone with God (Matthew 6:6)

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus' first teaching on prayer is that every disciple needs a secret place. Moses gave no rules for prayer and the prophets said little about it. It is Christ who teaches us to pray. Worship is no longer bound to places, yet each of us needs a fixed spot and a quiet time where Jesus can meet and teach us. That inner chamber is his schoolroom, and he wants it to be bright and inviting.

**Meet your Father there.** Jesus names the Father three times in these verses. The first thing in private prayer is to meet him. The light of the closet is the Father's face, and its air is his Father-love, so every request becomes simple, childlike trust.

**Shut the door.** God hides himself from the natural eye. While we are absorbed in our own thoughts and religious exercises, we miss him. Withdrawing from the world and its noise pictures, and helps us enter, the inner sanctuary where our spirit truly meets the Invisible One.

**Come as you are.** Many are kept from prayer because they feel cold, dark and prayerless. That is exactly why to go in. Think less about how little you bring and more about how much the Father wants to give. Tell him plainly how cold your heart is, and let his love warm it.

**Secret prayer is never wasted.** "Thy Father … shall reward thee openly." The blessing does not depend on strong feelings while praying but on the Father's love and power. Its fruit will show in our life before others.

**Your Father knows what you need.** We need not pile up words to persuade an unwilling God. This truth leads to quiet, thoughtful prayer and to confident perseverance: "Father, you know I need this." Sometimes stop asking and simply say, "My Father sees, hears and knows."

Dwell much behind the closed door. To be alone with the Father should be our highest joy.
