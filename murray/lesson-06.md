Title: "How much more?" or, The Infinite Fatherliness of God (Matthew 7:9-11)

To remove all doubt about answered prayer, Jesus appeals to what everyone knows: a father gladly hears his child. If sinful earthly fathers give good gifts, how much more will the Father in heaven? As far as God is above sinful man, so much surer is his answer.

**Live as a child, and you will pray as a child.** A child's request has power because of the relationship it comes from. A son who avoids his father's house, company and wishes, yet expects to get whatever he asks, will be disappointed. One whose joy is the father's love and honour will find it the father's joy to grant his requests. "As many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God." Childlike asking is inseparable from childlike living.

**The promises sit inside the life.** In the Sermon on the Mount, the prayer promises are woven into the precepts. They belong to children who are poor in spirit and pure in heart, who let their light shine, love their enemies, give and pray in secret, forgive, trust the Father for food and clothing while seeking his kingdom first, and do his will rather than merely saying "Lord, Lord."

**Not perfection, but a whole heart.** Does this discourage the weak? No. Children differ in age and strength. God does not demand flawless obedience, only honest, whole-hearted surrender to live as his child. Nothing more, and nothing less. Whoever takes the Sermon on the Mount as their guide will find growing freedom to claim its promises.

**Fill the heart with the Father's love.** Picture the best earthly father's tenderness, then multiply it beyond reckoning. Let the Spirit pour that love into your heart, and live in it always, not only when you want something.

The first lesson is also the highest: to say well, "Abba, Father." Whoever can truly say "Our Father which art in heaven" holds the key to all prayer.
