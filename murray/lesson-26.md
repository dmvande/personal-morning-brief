Title: "I have prayed for thee," or, Christ the Intercessor (Luke 22:32; John 16:26; Hebrews 7:25)

All spiritual growth comes from seeing more clearly what Jesus is to us. When we see that Christ is our life, the Christian life stops being a vain struggle and becomes resting in him. The same is true of prayer. It need not be strain or anxious care, because it is a sharing in Christ's own life of prayer.

**His intercession is real and essential.** Christ's priestly work on earth, like Aaron's, was only a beginning. Like Melchizedek, he now lives beyond the veil to intercede. "It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who … maketh intercession for us." Every conversion, every step of sanctification and every victory over sin flows from his asking and receiving from the Father. His mediation on the throne is as real and necessary as his work on the cross.

**We share in the work itself.** We are his body, and the head cannot say to the feet, "I have no need of thee." We share his life, his righteousness and his work, and his intercession too. His life in us remains a praying life. These are not two separate streams of prayer: what he prays passes through us, and what we pray passes through him. He is the angel with the golden censer, adding incense to the prayers of the saints.

**Christ is surety for our prayer life.** Many found a new beginning when they saw Christ as surety for their faithfulness. It is just as freeing to see him as the source of our praying, breathing his own prayerfulness into us. "I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not." His prayer doesn't make our faith unnecessary. It is the root of it.

**For others, not only ourselves.** These promises stay sealed while we pray mainly for ourselves. They open to fruitful branches, to disciples sent as Jesus was sent, who like a seed lose their life to multiply it. Find the souls entrusted to your prayers, make intercession for them your fellowship with God, and share his joy in saving people.
