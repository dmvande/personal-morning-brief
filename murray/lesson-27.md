Title: "Father, I will," or, Christ the High Priest (John 17:24)

After giving his unlimited promises about prayer, Jesus prays aloud. His high-priestly prayer shows what his intercession in heaven will be like, and it teaches us, as priests with him, how to intercede.

**He prays for himself.** "Father, glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee." He pleads the covenant, his finished work and his desire to glorify the Father, and asks boldly. *Our lesson:* to pray in his name is to pray in sympathy with him. Come before the Father in Christ. Plead his finished work, declare that we too live to finish our work for God's glory, and ask that the Son be glorified in us. Only when our own relationship with God is clear can we intercede well for others.

**He prays for his own circle.** These are the disciples the Father gave him, marked by receiving his word and now sent into the world as he was sent. He asks that they be kept from the evil one and sanctified through the truth. *Our lesson:* parents, teachers, pastors and every believer have a circle. Pray personally and specifically. First pray that they receive the word, and give it to them yourself. When they stumble, don't judge or give up. Pray, "Father, keep them; sanctify them."

**He prays for all believers.** His heart widens to embrace every place and age. He asks that they be one, as a witness to the world that he was sent, and that they be with him in glory. *Our lesson:* those who have proved prayer in their own circle will pray for the whole church's unity, in spirit and truth rather than outward organization.

**The manner: "Father, I will."** Jesus claimed the Father's promise, "Ask of me." Abiding in him, we may say the same about what God has promised. The words seem too bold at first, but they are given to a will wholly surrendered to his. Whoever loses his will finds it again, renewed with divine strength.

Throw off the sloth that passes for humility, and take up the calling to plead and prevail for others.
