Title: "What wilt thou?" or, Prayer Must Be Definite (Mark 10:51)

Jesus knew what blind Bartimaeus wanted, yet he asked, "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" The man was healed only after he named his need.

**Vague prayer misses the mark.** Many cry for mercy or blessing without saying what they want. Definite prayer makes us know our real needs, tests whether our desires are sincere and scriptural, and helps us recognize the answer when it comes. Murray compares it to a marksman who aims each shot instead of firing into a crowd.

**Start with your own circle.** Each believer has a family, friends and neighbours. Naming them before God brings us into the training-school of faith. Answers in specific matters make our broader prayers believing and effective.

**Family letters and business letters.** Prayer includes loving fellowship, but it also needs a "business" element: clear requests for which we expect a reply.

**Wishing is not willing.** Jesus asks what we will, not what we wish. Many prayers are passing wishes, soon forgotten. Willing means wanting it enough to persevere at any cost.

**A surrendered will is a strong will.** The prayer of faith finds God's will in a promise and pleads for it until it comes. A child who has yielded his will to the Father is trusted to will boldly. Claiming "no will" can be spiritual sloth dressed up as humility.
