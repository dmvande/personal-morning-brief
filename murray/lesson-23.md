Title: "Bear fruit, that the Father may give what ye ask," or, Obedience the Path to Power in Prayer (John 15:16; James 5:16)

Jesus chose and appointed us to bear lasting fruit, "that whatsoever ye shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you." Fruitfulness is the evidence of abiding, and those wholly consecrated to their calling are the ones granted influence in heaven's council.

**This does not contradict free grace.** John writes that we receive whatever we ask "because we keep his commandments." James says the prayer of a righteous man avails much. David appeals to the cleanness of his hands, and the Psalms often plead the integrity of the one praying. Jesus himself says, "Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you."

**Obedience comes first.** Our evangelical faith can focus on experiences to obtain in prayer. Scripture puts equal weight on obedience as the path to blessing. Our first stance toward God should be surrender to his glory and will, not merely how to enjoy his favour, where self may still be central. Service and obedience filled the Son's earthly life, and they must matter to us more than rest, light, joy or strength. In John 14–15 the Spirit, the Son's self-revelation, the Father's indwelling, Christ's love, his friendship and prevailing prayer all wait for the obedient. Faith and obedience are two sides of one surrender.

**We reversed the order.** We prayed for grace to bear fruit and wondered why answers didn't come. We wanted comfort and strength first so the work would feel easy. Jesus wants obedient fruit-bearing first, whether we feel weak or strong, and that path leads to prevailing prayer.

**It is right this way.** Those whose requests help steer God's work must be people who have learned obedience and whose loyalty is beyond question.

**Begin each day with "I belong to the Master."** Act in every moment as his property, his servant and his friend. Live to bless others and testify of his love, trusting him to give what he commands.
