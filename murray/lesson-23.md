Title: "Bear fruit, that the Father may give what ye ask," or, Obedience the Path to Power in Prayer (John 15:16)

Jesus chose us to bear lasting fruit, "that whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he may give it you." Those wholly given to their calling are the ones granted influence in heaven.

**This does not contradict free grace.** John writes that we receive whatever we ask "because we keep his commandments." James says the prayer of a righteous man avails much. The Psalms appeal to the integrity of the one praying. Jesus himself says, "Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you."

**Obedience comes first.** Evangelical faith can focus on experiences to obtain. Our first stance toward God, though, should be surrender to his glory and will. Service and obedience filled the Son's life, and they are the path to every higher blessing. In John 14–15, the Spirit, the Son's self-revelation, the Father's indwelling, Christ's friendship and prevailing prayer all wait for the obedient.

**We reversed the order.** We asked for comfort and strength first so the work would feel easy. Jesus wants obedient fruit-bearing first, whether we feel weak or strong, and that path leads to prevailing prayer.

**It is right this way.** Those whose requests help steer God's work must be people whose loyalty is beyond question.

Begin each day with "I belong to the Master." Live to bless others, and trust him to give what he commands.
