Title: "In my Name," or, The All-Prevailing Plea (John 14:13-14; 15:16; 16:23-26)

The disciples had never asked in Christ's name, and he had never used the phrase. In his farewell words he repeats it again and again, attached to unlimited promises. His name is our only plea, and it is enough.

**What a name means.** A name calls up the whole person: his character, honour and power. To act in someone's name is to come with his authority as his representative. No one gives free use of his name without trusting that his honour and interests are safe. Taking another's name means setting aside our own.

**Three pictures of the union behind the name.**
- *A business union.* A merchant leaving home gives a devoted chief clerk power to draw large sums in his name, for the firm's interests only. Christ left his kingdom's work to his servants, and they can use his name to the degree they live wholly for his interests.
- *A life union.* A child bears his father's name because he shares his life, and others honour him for it as long as his character matches. Our power with God, people and devils matches our spiritual union with Jesus.
- *A love union.* A poor bride takes her husband's name and buys freely in it. Bearing another's name means giving up our own independent life and gaining all that the name holds.

**Not a messenger for an absent master.** Jesus is with the Father, not absent. To ask in his name is to ask in full union of interest, life and love with him.

**The name rules the life.** "Do all in the name" and "ask all in the name" go together. Scripture speaks of walking in God's name and of those ready to die for the name of the Lord Jesus. God looks at our life, not our lips. When the name is everything to us, it will obtain everything for us.

**The promise guards itself.** No one can use the name beyond what they actually live. Use it boldly to open heaven's treasures for a perishing world.
