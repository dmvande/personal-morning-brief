Title: "My words in you," or, The Word and Prayer (John 15:7)

A newly converted man put it simply: "I pray: I speak to my Father. I read: my Father speaks to me." Prayer is a dialogue, not a monologue.

**The Word surrounds prayer.** Before prayer it shows what the Father invites us to ask. During prayer it gives faith its warrant and plea. After prayer it brings the Father's answer. God's hearing of us depends on our hearing of him. What his words are to us is the test of what he himself is to us.

**His words are himself.** Elsewhere Jesus says "ye in me, and I in you." Here he says "ye in me, and my words in you." His words abiding in us are equivalent to his abiding in us. A person reveals himself in his words: in promises he gives himself, and in commands he shares his will. When God speaks, he gives himself, his love, life, will and power, to those who receive his words.

**As we hear, so we speak.** Children who cannot hear struggle to learn to speak. Anyone can recite requests and promises, but praying words that reach God and move the unseen world depends on hearing his voice. That means more than thoughtful study, which can happen with little fellowship. It means reading in the Father's presence, under the Spirit's leading, where the Word comes as his living voice. Obedience and faith both flow from that personal presence.

**Life shapes prayer.** The Old Testament saints prayed "Do as thou hast said" and "according to thy word," and they obeyed "as the Lord had spoken." What God spoke, they heard and did. What they spoke, God heard and did. If I do what God says, God will do what I say.

**The real reason.** We have excused weak prayer as submission to God's wisdom when our own weak life was the cause. Nothing makes strong Christians but the word that comes from God's mouth. Let Christ's words fill heart and life, and his promise will become our experience.
