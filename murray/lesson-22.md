Title: "My words in you," or, The Word and Prayer (John 15:7)

A new convert put it simply: "I pray: I speak to my Father. I read: my Father speaks to me." Prayer is a dialogue, not a monologue.

**The Word surrounds prayer.** Before prayer it shows what to ask. During prayer it gives faith its warrant and plea. After prayer it brings the Father's answer. God's hearing of us depends on our hearing of him.

**His words are himself.** Elsewhere Jesus says "ye in me, and I in you." Here he says "ye in me, and my words in you." His words abiding in us are equivalent to his abiding in us. In his promises God gives himself to be held. In his commands he shares his will.

**As we hear, so we speak.** Children who cannot hear struggle to learn to speak. Anyone can recite requests and promises, but praying words that reach God depends on hearing his voice. That means more than studying the Bible. It means reading in the Father's presence, where the Word comes as his living voice. This personal presence produces obedience and faith.

**Life shapes prayer.** The Old Testament saints prayed "Do as thou hast said" and obeyed "as the Lord had spoken." If I do what God says, God will do what I say.

Our weak prayers usually come from weak lives, not from humble submission. Let Christ's words fill heart and life, and his promise will become our experience.
