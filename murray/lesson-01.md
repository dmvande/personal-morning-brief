Title: "Lord, teach us to pray," or, The Only Teacher (Luke 11:1)

The disciples had watched Jesus pray, and they saw how his public power flowed from his hidden life of prayer. So they asked him to teach them. Murray invites us to make the same request and enroll in the school Christ keeps open for anyone who wants to learn to pray. He works through the petition one word at a time.

**"To pray."** Prayer is simple enough for a child, yet it is the highest work a person can do: fellowship with God, the channel of every blessing, the means by which God's promises, his kingdom and his glory come. We easily settle for the form of prayer without its power, so we need to be taught.

**"Us."** The promises and power the saints knew in prayer are meant for ordinary believers too, and Christ's work depends on our prayer.

**"Teach."** Prayer seems easy at first and grows harder. To be effective it must seek God's glory, surrender to his will, trust him fully, be offered in Jesus' name and persevere. These are learned through practice and a teachable spirit.

**"Lord."** No one teaches like Jesus. He is still praying, and by his Spirit he breathes prayer into us. Jesus never taught his disciples to preach, only to pray. Power with God comes before power with people.
