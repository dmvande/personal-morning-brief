Title: "Lord, teach us to pray," or, The Only Teacher (Luke 11:1)

The disciples had watched Jesus pray and saw how his public power flowed from his hidden life of prayer. They knew no one prayed like him, so they asked him to teach them. Murray invites us to make the same request and enroll anew in the school Christ keeps open for all who long to learn the divine art of prayer and intercession. He then works through the petition one word at a time.

**"To pray."** Prayer is simple enough for a child, yet it is the highest work a person can do: fellowship with the unseen, holy God. It is the channel of every blessing and the secret of power and life, for ourselves, for others, for the church and for the world. God's promises wait on it for their fulfillment, and his kingdom for its coming. Yet we easily rest in the form of prayer while its power is missing. Training, habit and emotion can produce prayer with no spiritual force.

**"Us."** The saints of old prayed with great power, and if that was so under the Old Covenant, how much more now. The promises and gifts of heaven are for ordinary believers too. Christ's work, the coming of his kingdom and the glory of his name all depend in part on our prayer.

**"Teach."** Prayer seems easy at first and grows harder. We do not know how to pray as we ought. True prayer must seek God's glory, surrender to his will, trust him fully, be offered in Jesus' name and persevere. All this is learned only in the school of much prayer and with a teachable spirit.

**"Lord."** No one teaches like Jesus. He learned prayer through earthly trials, and in heaven his life is still prayer. By his Spirit he breathes prayer into us. He never taught his disciples to preach, only to pray, because power with God comes before power with people.
