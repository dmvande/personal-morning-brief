Title: "Ask, and it shall be given you," or, The Certainty of the Answer to Prayer (Matthew 7:7-8; James 4:3)

Next to the Father's love, the most important lesson in the school of prayer is this: everyone who asks receives. Jesus repeats the promise six ways because he knows how natural doubt is to us, and how easily we treat prayer as a religious duty with no answer expected.

**Ask, seek, knock.** These may describe a progression. Asking receives the gift. Seeking finds the Giver himself. Knocking gains entrance to abiding fellowship in the Father's home. None of them is in vain.

**Take the words as given.** Jesus spoke unconditionally on purpose. Don't weaken his promise with human reasoning, or demand that every difficulty be settled before believing it. Accept it first, and he will explain it in time. Write it on the walls of your inner chamber: "Every one that asketh receiveth."

**Prayer has two halves.** Asking is our part and giving is God's, and they belong together. As a child proves a sum by checking the answer, answered prayer is the proof that we have prayed rightly. When no answer comes, it is easier for the flesh to call it submission than to let the Spirit search us and purify our praying. Murray calls the slothful "resignation" that settles for no answer a mark of a diseased Christian life.

**Answered prayer should be normal.** Many Christians pray daily without knowing definite answers as the rule of life. The Father wants daily fellowship with his children through distinct requests and distinct answers, as the psalmists knew.

**Even "no" is an answer.** Moses asked to enter Canaan and Jesus prayed in Gethsemane, and each was clearly told God's will. Our God is not silent like the idols. We either withdraw a request that is not his will or persevere until the answer comes.

Don't let our feeble experience become the measure of what faith may expect. Trust Jesus to teach us to pray so that the answer can come.
