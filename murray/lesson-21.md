Title: "If ye abide in me," or, The All-Inclusive Condition (John 15:7)

God's promises and their conditions go together. The unlimited promise "ask whatsoever ye will" rests on one condition: "if ye abide in me." The Father always hears Christ, and we are heard by being in him.

**Don't rewrite the promise.** Faced with many unanswered prayers, believers often add "if it be God's will," a qualifier Jesus didn't add. Murray urges us instead to admit that our failure to abide is the real explanation, and to let that drive us to seek full abiding.

**Abiding grows in stages.**
- *Faith.* The young believer simply trusts that abiding is possible and is his duty, despite his failures.
- *Obedience.* He discovers that faith must show itself in keeping Christ's commands. The aim becomes a will united with Christ's.
- *Union of life.* Still something is missing: deeper impulses beyond the reach of the will. Faith and obedience lead to the promised fullness, in which the Spirit comes, the Son reveals himself, and Father and Son make their home in us. "Ye in me, and I in you" becomes a union of life and nature, not only of will.

**Then the promise is natural.** When Christ dwells in our will and prays in us by his Spirit, what we ask will be done.

The church's weakness in the world comes from shallow abiding. Don't be discouraged, though. Look to Christ himself more than to the abiding, and press on to know it fully.
