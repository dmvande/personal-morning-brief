Title: "If ye abide in me," or, The All-Inclusive Condition (John 15:7)

God's promises and their conditions go together. "Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you." The unlimited promise "ask what ye will" rests on one condition: "if ye abide in me." The Father always hears Christ, and we are heard by being in him.

**Don't rewrite the promise.** Faced with many unanswered prayers, believers often add "if it be God's will," a qualifier Jesus didn't add. That protects both God's honour and their own. Murray urges us instead to admit that our failure to abide is the real explanation, and to let our weakness in prayer drive us to seek full abiding.

**Abiding grows in stages.** Like many of God's words, "abide in me" keeps opening to deeper meaning as we grow.
- *Faith.* The young believer simply trusts that, despite failures, abiding is his duty and within reach. He fixes his eyes on the Saviour's love and faithfulness.
- *Obedience.* He then finds that faith must show itself in keeping Christ's commands: "If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love." Self-will once overlooked comes to light. The aim becomes a will wholly united with Christ's.
- *Union of life.* Even then something is missing. The will is on Christ's side, yet the flesh still stirs in depths the will cannot reach, and much beauty of holiness is lacking. Faith and obedience lead to the promised fullness: the Spirit comes, the Son reveals himself, and Father and Son make their home in us. "Ye in me, and I in you" becomes a union of life and nature, not only of will.

**Then the promise is natural.** When Christ dwells in our love, will and life and prays in us by his Spirit, what we ask will be done.

The church's weakness before unbelief and worldliness comes from shallow abiding. Don't be discouraged. Focus less on the abiding than on Christ himself, and press on: "Not as though I had already attained."
