Title: "At that day," or, The Holy Spirit and Prayer (John 16:23-26; Jude 20-21)

John writes to little children, young men and fathers. Jesus' teaching on prayer moves through similar stages. In the Sermon on the Mount, children learn to trust the Father. Later comes a stage of conflict ("this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting"). In the farewell discourse, mature friends receive "whatsoever ye will."

**"At that day" means Pentecost.** "Hitherto ye have asked nothing in my name." Power in prayer in Jesus' name was to be the mark of the Spirit's coming to dwell in believers.

**Why the Spirit makes the difference.** The Spirit is the bond of the eternal giving and asking between Father and Son. Christ now lives to intercede, applying in each believer the victory he won on the cross. The Spirit came from the glorified Jesus as something new, bringing the full fruit of finished redemption.

**We are drawn into Christ's prayer.** Through the Spirit, sometimes without words, we are caught up into the stream of Christ's continual intercession. His prayers become ours and ours become his. So we have direct access: "the Father himself loveth you."

**The one great need.** Believers need to be filled with the Spirit of the glorified Jesus. That means more than conversion or occasional influence. It means his Spirit in our whole life, not just our prayer times.

What our prayer accomplishes depends on what we are. Living in Christ's name is the secret of praying in it.
