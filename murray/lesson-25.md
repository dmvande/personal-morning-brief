Title: "At that day," or, The Holy Spirit and Prayer (John 16:23-26; Jude 20-21)

John writes to little children, young men and fathers, three stages of the Christian life. Jesus' teaching on prayer moves through similar stages. In the Sermon on the Mount, children learn to trust the Father. Later comes a stage of conflict: "this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting." In the farewell discourse, mature friends who know his secrets receive the keys of the kingdom: "whatsoever ye will."

**"At that day" means Pentecost.** "Hitherto have ye asked nothing in my name." Power in prayer in Jesus' name was to be a mark of the Spirit's coming to dwell in believers after Christ's victory on the cross and his ascension.

**Why the Spirit makes the difference.** The Spirit is the bond of the eternal giving and asking between Father and Son. Christ began reconciling God and humanity in his own body, and now he lives to intercede, applying that victory in each member. The Spirit "was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified." He came as something new: the Spirit of the glorified Jesus, bringing the full fruit of finished redemption, not only the Spirit of God known to Old Testament saints.

**We are drawn into Christ's prayer.** Through the Spirit, sometimes without words, we are caught up into the stream of Christ's continual intercession. His prayers become ours and ours become his. So we have direct access: "I say not that I will pray the Father for you: for the Father himself loveth you."

**The one great need.** Believers need to be filled with the Spirit of the glorified Jesus. That means more than conversion or occasional influence. It means his Spirit as the life of our whole walk, not just our prayer times. Then we learn what it is to pray always in the Spirit.

What our prayer accomplishes depends on what we are. Living in Christ's name is the secret of praying in it. As much as there is of Christ's Spirit in us, so much is there of real prayer.
