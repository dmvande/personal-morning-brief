Title: "How much more the Holy Spirit," or, The All-Comprehensive Gift (Luke 11:13)

In Luke, Jesus repeats his "how much more," but this time he names the gift: the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is the chief of the Father's gifts, the one that contains all the others, and the one he most delights to give.

**The highest gift a father can give.** A wise father wants above all to reproduce his own spirit in his child, so the child shares his mind and plans. God can give nothing higher than his own Spirit, the very life of God. At Jesus' baptism the Father's voice and the Spirit came together. Because we are sons, God sends the Spirit of his Son into our hearts. Jesus entered heaven by his blood to obtain this gift and send it down.

**The first object of prayer.** All fullness is in Jesus, and the Spirit's work is to make it ours in living experience. His names show how he meets every need: the Spirit of grace, faith, adoption, truth, prayer, judgment and burning, holiness, power and glory. A child of God needs one thing to live as a child: to be filled with him.

**Ask for more.** Believers already have the Spirit, yet like a branch full of sap that keeps drawing more to ripen its fruit, we keep asking for his fuller possession and moment-by-moment guidance. God's promise to pour out his Spirit and his command "be filled with the Spirit" set the measure of our expectation.

**Receive by faith, not feelings.** As we pray, we take the gift in faith and say, "I have what I asked." Then we continue in thankful, believing prayer until the blessing breaks through and fills our whole being. Such prayer does not only ask and hope. It takes and holds.

**Then pray for others.** Those who have learned this for themselves gain freedom and power to pray for the Spirit to be poured out on the church, on individuals and on every work of God.
